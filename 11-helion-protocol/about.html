Manifesto · founded 2023

AI training shouldn't run on three companies' infrastructure.

We built Helion because three cloud providers cannot remain the chokepoint for the most important computational substrate of this decade. A more permissionless, more economically fair, more geographically distributed alternative was inevitable. We are trying to ship the version of it that doesn't suck.

In 2022 and 2023, the three of us watched the same thing happen from three different vantage points. Aiden was a senior engineer at CoreWeave during the H100 supply crunch, watching seed-stage AI startups get billed $40,000 a month for compute their model wasn't even using efficiently. Vera was running a Solana validator network and saw the obvious analogy: compute is becoming the new gas. Yusuf was at Filecoin building proofs of storage and started asking, in March 2023, why we couldn't prove computation the same way we prove storage.

The three of us met in Lisbon in May 2023. The thesis we converged on was straightforward:

1. Two-thirds of GPU capacity is sitting idle. Render farms, research clusters, individual workstations. The combined idle compute is roughly five times AWS's published AI capacity. The market exists. The matching layer does not.

2. Verifiable compute is now feasible. The combination of TEEs (which were exotic in 2018, mainstream in 2023) and zk-SNARKs (which dropped to < 200ms verification in late 2022) makes proof-of-compute a thing you can actually build, not a thing you have to handwave around.

3. The economics of cloud compute have to break. AWS gross margin on AI compute is ~62%. That margin is going to be competed away. We'd rather it be competed away by an open protocol than by a fourth hyperscaler.

Helion is what we built. A custom L1, optimized for compute settlement. zk-verifiable execution inside TEE enclaves. A 70/25/5 economic split, paid in USDC. The protocol is permissionless to join, permissionless to consume, and audited twice by Trail of Bits.

We will not become an AWS replacement next year. We may not even be one in five years. But we believe the idea of a compute substrate that is verifiable, distributed, and economically fair is correct — and we believe enough of the right people are now working on it that the version that wins will be the version with the best engineering and the most honest economic design.

We hope that's us. If it isn't, we'll have written enough open-source primitives along the way that the thing that does win will look something like Helion.

— Vera, Aiden, Yusuf · Lisbon, July 2023 · revised October 2025
