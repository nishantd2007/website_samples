About Helix

We built the wealth platform we'd been trying to use.

Aanya was an executive at Goldman who watched her own ISO exercise turn into a six-figure AMT bill no one had modeled for her. Lucas ran finance at Stripe and helped two hundred engineers navigate the same vesting calendar from spreadsheets. They started Helix in 2022.

The wealth management industry is two things at once: extremely good at serving people with $50M+, and shockingly bad at serving the people who will eventually have $50M.

If you are a senior engineer at a fast-growing private company with $2M in vesting equity, a four-comma annual compensation, and no time to think about any of it — your options are: Charles Schwab (a 1990s product), Wealthfront (a 60/40 ETF and a chatbot), a Morgan Stanley FA (your account number begins with seven zeros and your advisor is in a phone bank), or a family office (you don't have $25M yet, so no).

None of these are what you actually want. What you want is one app, one fee, one advisor who knows your equity, and a tax engine that knows what an ISO is. That is Helix.

We are not, and will never be, the cheapest. We are not, and will never be, the biggest. We are built for the ten years between your first significant comp and your eventual liquidity — the years that are the hardest to navigate and the most expensive to navigate wrongly.

— Aanya & Lucas, New York, March 2022

Investment philosophy

Six things we believe, in plain English.

01

Time in the market beats timing it.

We don't pick stocks. We don't time entries. The compounding work is done by allocation discipline and tax efficiency — not by being clever.

02

Concentration is a tax decision.

Holding 60% of your net worth in your employer's stock is a position. Diversifying into the index isn't moralism — it's tax-loss harvesting plus risk reduction in one move.

03

After-tax is the only return.

We compare your portfolio to the S&P 500 after estimated taxes on the S&P's gains. Most reporting hides the tax line. We surface it on every dashboard.

04

Fees compound too.

30 basis points is a deliberate price. The category averages 110. The difference, compounded over thirty years, is a second house.

05

Equity comp deserves its own product.

RSUs, ISOs, NSOs, ESPPs, 83(b)s, AMT, QSBS — most platforms treat these as edge cases. For our clients, they're the main case.

06

A real person, not a bot.

Money is emotional. A chatbot cannot read your tone when you call about an exit, a layoff, a new baby. Your CFP can.
