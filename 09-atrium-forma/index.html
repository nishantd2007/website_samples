A note from the studio

A building should make less noise than its function requires.

We are an eleven-person architecture studio working primarily on libraries, chapels, museums, and small institutional buildings in northern Europe and Japan. Lukas Verhoeven and Hana Mori founded the studio in 2014 after five years together at SANAA in Tokyo.

Atrium Forma has completed fourteen buildings in its first decade. Five are libraries, three are museums, four are chapels, two are schools. We were finalists for the 2024 Mies van der Rohe Award and we don't take more than three new commissions per year.

The approach
01 / Listen

The brief is the building, before drawings.

Every project begins with three to six months in conversation with the client, the site, and the neighbors. We draw nothing until we know what we are listening to.

02 / Restraint

Less, when less serves the brief.

We resist gestures that exist only for the photograph. A library should disappear behind the books. A chapel should disappear behind the light.

03 / Material

Two or three materials, well done.

Most of our buildings are built from two to three primary materials. Concrete and cedar. Brick and oak. Limestone and steel. The discipline is the architecture.

04 / Stay

We are there for ten years after handover.

Atrium Forma maintains every building it has completed. We return annually for the first decade, free of charge. We watch how the building ages.

Commissions

Three commissions a year. No more.

We accept three new commissions per year. The studio is closed to new work between commissions four and eleven. If you are considering us for a project, begin with an unhurried first letter.
