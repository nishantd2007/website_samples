About Aurelian

A great house is a long story. Most brokerages tell it badly.

Aurelian was founded by an editor, not a salesperson. The brokerage represents eight to twelve significant houses each year — capped on purpose — and every property is documented with the care of a magazine feature.

Margaux Whitfield spent the better part of a decade running the architecture desk at Dwell. She edited two hundred and forty house features. She fact-checked the provenance of every floorboard, every owner, every architect. She walked through a thousand houses by the end of it.

What she noticed: the real estate industry, even at the highest end, knew almost nothing about the houses it was selling. The listings were two paragraphs. The history was almost always wrong. The buyers — wealthy, smart, design-literate — were starved of the context they needed to fall in love with a place.

She founded Aurelian in 2018 with a single principle: the way you market a great house is the way Dwell would write about it. Long-form. Architecturally literate. Sourced. Honest about what isn't perfect.

Six years in, Aurelian represents eight to twelve houses a year — capped, because Margaux researches and writes every brief herself. Each property gets a 96-page printed monograph that buyers receive before the first walkthrough. The brokerage has closed $620M of transactions since founding, with a 72% repeat-or-referral rate.

The 4% commission is not the cheapest in the category. We are not trying to be. Half of it is donated to the local historical society of the property — a quiet practice we started in year one and never publicized until a journalist found it in 2023.

— Margaux Whitfield, Principal · East Hampton, October 2024

The methodology

Four steps. Six months, on average.

Most brokerages list a property in twelve hours. Aurelian takes six months between meeting a seller and sharing the brief with the first buyer. The work is the point.

01

Walkthrough & research

Three days on site. Archives. Town records. The original architect's library. The neighbor who's lived there longest.

02

The brief, written

A 96-page printed monograph. Provenance, restoration history, every architect named, every fault disclosed.

03

Photography

A three-day shoot with Maxime Boudreau or Stephen Kent Johnson. Print-quality, never iPhone-staged.

04

Private circulation

Briefs are mailed by hand to a vetted list of forty-eight collectors before any public listing is considered.

A quiet practice

Two percent of every fee, forever, to history.

In our first year we donated $2,400 to the East Hampton Historical Society in lieu of a holiday gift. We've done it every closing since — half of our four-percent fee, donated to the local historical society of the property.

To date the practice has placed $1.84M with twenty-three historical societies across New York and Colorado. We never asked anyone to publicize it; a journalist found it in our LLC filings in 2023.
