About Wanderlight

Started by two people who'd had enough of three-day cities.

Wanderlight was founded in Lisbon in March 2020 — a date that, in retrospect, was either the worst or the best timing imaginable. The thesis was simple, and the world about to lock down was about to prove it: slow is the only travel worth doing.

Anya Kasturi spent twelve years leading expeditions for National Geographic. Diego Velasquez trained as an anthropologist and ran cultural programs for a now-defunct hotel group. They met in 2017, in a noodle shop in Hanoi, after both of them had been quietly disgusted by the way the group above them had been treated by their respective guides.

Three years and many emails later, they founded Wanderlight with eight friends as a kind of guinea-pig cohort. The first trip — twenty-one days through the Sierra Madre with a poet, a cook, and the eight of them — sold out from the email list alone. The second sold out faster.

The thesis: travel had become an industry of seeing — see ten things in three days, post six of them, forget the other four. The good travel had always been about staying long enough to be slightly bored, slightly humbled, slightly changed. A morning where nothing happens is the morning you'll tell people about for the rest of your life.

Wanderlight runs eighteen trips a year. Each one between fourteen and thirty days. Each one capped at eight travelers. Each one led by a regional expert who lives there — a weaver, an archaeologist, a mezcalero, a Sherpa — paired with a Wanderlight host who walks every step with you.

The trips are not cheap. They are not luxurious either, in the spa-and-thread-count sense. They are generous — generous with time, with food, with relationships, with the kind of silence that only six other strangers around a fire can give you.

— Anya & Diego · Lisbon, March 2024 (four years in)

Six principles

Things we believe, after eighty-three trips.

01

Stay long enough to be ignored.

You haven't met a place until people there have stopped looking at you. That takes more days than most people give it.

02

One full day of nothing.

Every trip has at least one day with nothing on the schedule. Not a back-up, not a rest day — a genuine, planned, empty day.

03

The local is the guide.

Our hosts are facilitators. The real teacher on every trip is a person born twenty minutes from where you're standing.

04

Eat slowly.

Every dinner is a minimum of two hours, in one place, around one table. We don't move on for dessert.

05

No more than eight.

Above eight people, you stop seeing the place. You start managing the group. We cap at eight. Often six.

06

Leave something behind.

Three percent of every trip's price funds a local project chosen by the host. To date, $478,000 placed across nine regions.

The collective

Anya, Diego, and a tribe of nineteen regional experts.

Anya and Diego host roughly half the trips themselves. The other half are co-led by Wanderlight hosts trained over a two-year apprenticeship.

Every region has a permanent expert who designs the itinerary, opens the doors, knows the village. Pemba Sherpa in the Khumbu, Nilda Callañaupa in Cusco, Yasmin Hafez in the Western Desert, Don Bonifacio in Oaxaca, and fifteen more.

We are eleven people in the studio. We have no plans to be more.
