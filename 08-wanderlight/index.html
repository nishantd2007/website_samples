The philosophy

Most travel today is built around density — see ten things in three days. Wanderlight is built around the opposite. We move at the pace of a place. We linger. We arrive in a village and stay until people start to know us.

Every trip has at least one full day with nothing scheduled. Not because we ran out of ideas — because that is the day you'll remember.

A sample, day by day

What twenty-one days in the Andes actually looks like.

Day 01

Cusco, settling.

You arrive. We hand you a bag of coca tea and an instruction to walk no further than five blocks for the first 36 hours. Dinner together at a quiet courtyard table.

Day 04

Pisac market, before tourists.

We are the first ones there. You meet the weaver who will accompany us for the rest of the trip. She picks the textiles she wants you to understand.

Day 09

Chinchero village, a slow week.

Seven nights in a single home in a 6,000-foot village. Mornings with the loom. Afternoons walking. One day with nothing scheduled — you find yours.

Day 16

Q'eswachaka rope bridge.

The annual rebuilding of the last Incan rope bridge — only locals attend. A private viewing arranged through five years of relationships.

Day 21

Last meal, slow goodbye.

A long lunch in a high-altitude orchard. You leave knowing eleven people's names and having promised three of them you'll write.
