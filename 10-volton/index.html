Signature feature · The Voice

A 1962 DB4, re-recorded for electric.

Electric cars are quiet — to the point of soulless. Volton's powertrain is paired with a closed-circuit acoustic system: two microphones, four bone-conduction transducers, and a generative model trained on a 1962 Aston Martin DB4 GT.

The "voice" responds in real time to throttle position, motor torque, and chassis pitch — and it is not piped through speakers. It is conducted through the chassis itself, so the cabin feels the way an internal combustion grand tourer used to feel, without producing any external sound.

The car is silent to the street. It is alive to its driver.

The workshop · Stuttgart

Twelve people. Four bays. Twenty-five vehicles a year.

Each Volton One spends eight weeks in the Stuttgart workshop. Eight hands per vehicle. The carbon monocoque is laid up by Henrik, the same engineer who built the 2002 Carrera GT chassis. The motor wiring is hand-soldered by Sayid, who came to us from Pininfarina.
