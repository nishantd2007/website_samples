Manifesto · 2022 — present

A grand tourer should not have died with the engine.

Most electric hypercars to date have been quarter-mile drag specials. Volton is the opposite: a 2+2 GT designed for an unhurried 2,000 km across the Alps with a friend in the right seat. Quiet on the outside. Felt on the inside. Built one at a time.

We met at Rimac in 2019. Lina had come from Porsche's Mission E team, where she'd watched the Taycan move from a clean-sheet electric grand tourer concept into something heavier, more performance-fixated, more dragstrip than driving road. Ravi had come from Stuttgart's Tier-1 supplier ecosystem and was building powertrains for everyone — Lotus, Pininfarina, Rimac. We left in 2022 because we agreed on a thesis no one was building toward.

The grand tourer is the most important automotive typology of the last century — long bonnet, intimate cabin, the kind of car you point at the south of France with two bags. It is the typology electrification has most damaged. Tesla doesn't build GTs. Lucid is sedans. Rimac is hypercars. Aston Martin and Ferrari are still on V12s. The category, as a category, is empty.

Volton is our attempt at the missing GT. Three principles:

1. Quiet to the world, alive to the driver. External pedestrian sound is below 42 dB. Internal cabin sound is mapped from a 1962 DB4. The car is silent to a microphone three feet from it. The driver feels the car in the chassis itself.

2. Hand-built or not at all. Twelve people in Stuttgart. Twenty-five cars a year. The Carbon monocoque is laid up by Henrik Mayer. The motor wiring is hand-soldered by Sayid Suleiman. The interior is fitted by Anna Schmidt. We employ eight other people whose names belong on the same line.

3. Ninety-nine cars, then stop. The series cap is 99. After the 99th car, the workshop closes. We will not build a second model. We will not build a Volton SUV. We will not be acquired by Geely.

The Volton One is $1.4M base. The reservation deposit is $25,000, fully refundable. There are 99 allocations and 28 are open as of this writing. First deliveries are Q4 2027.

— Lina & Ravi · Zurich, March 2022 · revised October 2024

Six rules

Things we wrote on the workshop wall in March 2022.

01 / Series

Ninety-nine, then stop.

The series cap is 99 cars over four years. There is no Volton Two. The workshop closes after the 99th delivery.

02 / Sound

Quiet to the world.

External cabin sound below 42 dB at 50 km/h. The acoustic system is conducted internally only. You don't hear us coming.

03 / Hands

Twelve hands per car.

Eight people touch every Volton One, with their hands, by name. They are listed on the chassis plate of each vehicle.

04 / Material

Honest material, hand-finished.

Carbon, aluminum, leather, wool, brass, oak. No piano black. No fake-titanium chrome. Twenty hours of paint per car.

05 / Owners

The owner is the customer.

You will own your Volton One outright. You can sell it. You can race it. You can modify it. We are not Apple.

06 / After

Service forever.

Volton commits to lifetime parts and labor on every car. The Stuttgart workshop will remain open for service after the production cap.
