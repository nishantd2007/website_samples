Notre histoire

A bistro built from the ground up.

Mirepoix opened in October 2019 in a 1908 textile mill that had stood empty for eleven years. Six months of plaster, walnut, and patience. Then a kitchen.

Camille grew up in Lac-Mégantic, learning to make stock before she learned to ride a bike. Her grandmother kept a stove going all day on the back of the wood-fired range — onions sweating in butter, carrots and celery softening into them, a chicken carcass slowly giving up its perfume. Mirepoix. The base of everything good.

After culinary school in Quebec City, Camille spent six years in Paris under Hélène Darroze — first on the pass, then on cold apps, then finally with her own station. She came home in 2018 looking for a building that was already a story before she walked in. The Saint-Henri mill, with its rusted skylights and original soaking troughs, was that story.

Théo Marchand, her partner in the dining room, joined a month before opening. He brought the wine list, the vinyl crate, and an absolute refusal to ever let a table feel rushed.

Foundations, daily

A loaf at 6am, a stock at 8.

Every morning begins with bread and stock. Pâtissier Léa folds her starter while Théo grinds beans for the team’s espresso. By 11, the kitchen smells like home — if home were a slightly chaotic French grandmother’s.

Nothing on the plate begins after 4pm. If it’s not made from scratch by midday, it doesn’t make it onto the carte.

A fifty-mile pantry

Sourced inside a fifty-mile circle.

We work with eleven producers within an hour’s drive. The radish on your plate was pulled this morning. The cream was churned Tuesday. The duck was hand-raised by Marc in Saint-Eustache, the same farm Camille’s grandmother used to drive to.

If we can’t taste the place a thing was grown, it doesn’t come through the back door.
