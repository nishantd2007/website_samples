Manifesto

The next decade of infra looks nothing like the last.

In 2024 we left jobs at Datadog and Stripe to chase a single observation: the AI agents people are putting into production are running in the dark. There is no stack trace. There is no log line that means anything. A request leaves the user, hits seven LLM calls, three tool invocations, two silent retries, one failed JSON parse — and the only signal you get back is that the answer was wrong, sometimes.

Traditional observability was built for deterministic systems. Microservices that returned the same answer when you replayed the request. AI agents are stochastic, expensive, non-local, and increasingly autonomous. The tools we built between 2010 and 2022 do not work.

We believe the next decade of platform engineering will be defined by a new question: how do you operate a system that thinks? Tracing for agents. Evals as a deploy gate. Replay as a debugger. Cost as a first-class signal. Not after the fact — at the moment the agent is making the decision.

Nullspace is our attempt at that platform. We are radically open about how it works (we publish our schema, our OTLP exporter is MIT, our self-hosted runtime is available), and equally opinionated about how it should be used. We will not ship a feature that makes it easier to hide what your agent is doing. Every line of the product is built around exposing it.

We are six people. We are profitable. We will stay small for as long as we can afford to.

— Kai & Lena, Brooklyn / Lisbon, April 2024

What we believe

Eight non-negotiables.

01

Your data is yours

Traces are exportable to OTLP at any time. We will never hold your data hostage.

02

SDK first, dashboard second

If we can't make the SDK feel right in 90 seconds, the dashboard doesn't matter.

03

Show the prompt

Never hide what was sent to the model. Privacy controls live in the SDK, not in the UI.

04

No vendor lock-in

OpenTelemetry schema. Standard exporters. Self-hosting available from day one.

05

Per-seat pricing is a tax

You pay for spans, not for the number of people who want to read them.

06

Eval as CI

Regressions on the golden set block the deploy. No exceptions. No "I'll fix it after."

07

Sane defaults, always

If the default config is wrong, that's our bug. Configuration is not a feature.

08

Stay small

Twelve people, max. The day we hit thirteen is the day we stop being good at this.
